# The Ruler Problem

**Week 3 assignment — GENED 1201: Foundations of NeuroAI, Harvard (Fall 2026)**

An interactive page about a question people ask as though it were factual:
**have we reached AGI?**

It isn't factual. Before you can answer it, two things have to be settled that
nobody agrees on — what general intelligence *is*, and how you would measure it.
Your answer to the first decides which rulers are even valid, which decides the
verdict. People argue about the verdict; the disagreement is one floor down.

This page makes that dependency visible. You choose an account of general
intelligence, and watch the verdict change while the evidence stays exactly
the same.

## What's in it

- Three competing psychological accounts of why cognitive abilities correlate
  with one another: a common capacity, overlapping processes, and mutual
  reinforcement during development
- Four proposed rulers for machine intelligence: Morris et al. (2023),
  Hendrycks et al. (2025), Burnell et al. (2026), and Chollet (2019)
- A fourth position that rejects the question as the wrong shape for the object
- A fixed evidence panel that never changes, only its reading does
